Command Center

a confidential high-volume coaching business

Eight disconnected tools, collapsed into one operating system that maintains itself — and finds the leak, not just shows a dashboard.

A confidential high-volume coaching business ran six departments out of scattered Sheets and a half-dozen SaaS tools, with no shared view of a customer and no reliable way to see what was working. We replaced that with a self-refreshing operating system that pulls live data from every tool every hour, scores every customer and every call, surfaces exactly where customers stop finding value, and pushes each day's priorities to the team that owns them. The team runs it on AI; we handed it over documented.

(01)

The problem

Behind the sale sat six functions — and the data describing a single customer was smeared across eight systems that didn't talk.

No 360° view of a customer. Answering “who is this, what did they buy, did they show up, are they at risk?” meant a human opening five tabs and stitching it together by hand — every morning.

Refund prevention was manual and late. A large share of paying customers never logged in at all — a screaming refund signal nobody was systematically catching.

KPIs were unknowable. Leadership couldn't reliably say whether the refund rate was improving, or whether last month's changes helped or hurt.

Trackers rotted. The Sheets that ran daily work were stale the moment someone stopped updating them — bloated with resolved rows and duplicates.

(02)

What we built

One self-refreshing operating system that is both the single source of truth and the daily worklist for the whole business.

A live value ladder for every customer. Each paying customer sits in exactly one state — never logged in, logged in but no value yet, reached value, or refunded/left — with a refund-risk score from a grounded, audited model. One number, everywhere it's shown.

An activation funnel that finds the leak. Every milestone from payment to delivered outcome, as a share of paying customers — so the single biggest drop-off stage is obvious at a glance instead of buried. The surface names where value is lost, not just that it's lost.

Department operating tabs. Leadership scorecard, onboarding, community, delivery ops, refund desk and call review — each mirroring how that team actually works, each with its own live worklist.

AI graders on autopilot. An all-calls grader scores every call for effectiveness and sentiment, hourly; a refund post-mortem engine reconciles every system to explain why a specific customer refunded.

Self-maintaining trackers + push, not pull. Trackers seed themselves from live signals, advance status automatically and archive resolved rows; daily digests fire into the right Slack channel — booking misses to the SDR, onboarding lag to the onboarding lead, refund saves to the refund desk.

(03)

How it works

The elegance is in the operating model, not just the features.

A surface that rebuilds itself. Scheduled jobs fetch live data, re-run the scoring and regenerate the pages hourly — the header reads “live” with the current timestamp because staying current is the machine's job, not a person's.

One scoring model, reused everywhere. Refund risk, customer status and KPI logic live in shared single-source-of-truth modules, so the number a rep sees on a customer card is the number leadership sees on the scorecard.

Verified, not vibes. Wherever a claim could be self-reported — “we saved this refund,” “the team replied” — the system checks it against the authoritative source instead of trusting the label.

The takeaway

The win wasn't a dashboard — a dashboard rots. It was collapsing eight disconnected systems and six manual tracker cultures into one operating surface that scores itself hourly, surfaces exactly where customers stop finding value, and pushes the right slice to the right person — then handing it over so the team runs it on AI without us.

Honest data over flattering data

The first keyword research had been run on a source that inflated search volume roughly 3× and badly understated difficulty — a median KD of 12 against a real 47. Acting on it would have meant chasing traffic that didn't exist against competition we couldn't beat.

Rebuilt on data that holds. We rebuilt the entire keyword universe on Ahrefs as the primary source, kept the old data only as a cross-check, and ran a second full re-sweep two weeks later to confirm the numbers held.

Found the actual opening. The head terms are locked — ~2.18M searches/mo sit behind a wall a DR-0 site can't climb. But the event and matchup layer scores KD 0–11: ~330k searches/mo genuinely winnable now, with a defensible 45k–80k visits/mo target.

(02)

The engine we built

The thesis: a programmatic content engine on the wide-open event layer, not a head-term arm-wrestle — and a pipeline the team runs without us.

A programmatic content thesis. Win the event/matchup layer with pages that match the product's real strength, instead of an unwinnable fight for locked head terms.

A 24-piece, E-E-A-T content architecture. A clean URL structure — /learn/ explainers, /compare/ head-to-heads, platform hubs, state-by-state legal pages — with thin affiliate ideas steered out as YMYL risks and replaced with link-earning assets, including an original-data study built to attract backlinks and AI citations.

An AI pipeline the team runs. A self-contained starter pack — content workflow, prompt library, a 'write like a human' de-slopping guide, an internal-linking playbook and a catalog of marketing plays — so the founder's team executes on AI directly, with us coaching the judgment calls.

(03)

Caught before it cost them

The technical audit came owner-assigned, with impact ratings, effort estimates and a sequenced roadmap — not just a list.

A launch-blocking bug, caught pre-spend. Every editorial post under /insights was inheriting the homepage's title, meta and Open Graph tags — meaning the entire content program would have been invisible to search before it began. Caught before the content budget was spent against it.

Foundations set. Trailing-slash redirect chains resolved, author/editorial-policy (E-E-A-T) signals added, and Search Console access established.
